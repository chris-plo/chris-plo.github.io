# Somewhere between here and Cancún

I'm sitting at the airport, waiting for my flight to Cancún. It's delayed a little, nothing particularly dramatic. The kind of delay that gives you just enough extra time to wander around the terminal, check your phone for the tenth time, buy something you probably didn't need and eventually settle into a seat.

Around me, people are doing the usual airport things. Someone is talking loudly on the phone. A child is running around. There are announcements coming from the speakers, most of which I don't really listen to anymore. Suitcases rolling across the floor, people looking at screens, someone eating a sandwich. I'm a little bored, which I suppose is part of the experience.

And I'm waiting too.

It's the first day of vacation. Two weeks away from work and the familiar rhythm of everyday life. I'm looking forward to the trip, to spending time with family, to changing scenery and having a few days where I don't have to think about the usual things.

At the same time, leaving home always makes me a little anxious. Leaving our cats for two weeks doesn't help. I know they'll be fine. We have everything arranged, and rationally, there's nothing to worry about. Still, I find myself thinking about them, about home, and about all the little things I leave behind when I travel.

This year hasn't been an easy one. We've had our share of loss and disappointment, and there have been periods when life felt a little harder than usual. I don't really feel like unpacking all of that today. Perhaps it's enough to acknowledge that it's been a year I'd like to leave behind with a little more distance.

Work has been a particularly interesting part of it. At the beginning of the year, I struggled. I wasn't meeting the expectations I had for myself, and I wasn't entirely sure I was finding my footing. But now, towards the end of the year, I look back and see things differently. I've learned a lot, overcome some of those difficulties and accomplished things I'm genuinely proud of.

I don't think I would have appreciated that as much without the rougher start.

It's easy to spend most of your time thinking about what comes next, what you still need to learn or what you could have done better. I rarely stop and look back. Sitting here today, though, I find myself doing exactly that, and it feels good.

I'm excited about next year. About the things we'll continue building at work and at home, about the plans we have and the ones we haven't even thought of yet. I don't know how everything will turn out, of course, but I feel much more comfortable with where I am than I did a few months ago.

Maybe that's part of what makes leaving for vacation feel particularly nice today. Not that everything is suddenly perfect, but that I can take a break without feeling like I'm constantly trying to catch up with myself.

The announcement for my flight will probably come soon. I'll collect my things, find my seat and eventually watch Madrid disappear beneath the clouds. For now, I'm sitting here listening to voices over the speakers, watching strangers go about their own journeys and enjoying the fact that, for the next two weeks, I don't have to be anywhere in particular.

Well, except Cancún. Eventually.
